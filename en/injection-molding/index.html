WHAT IT IS

Mould making and injection moulding at HyperFab

Injection moulding is the main process for mass-producing plastic parts. Molten polymer is injected under pressure into a steel mould, cools, and the part comes out with repeatable dimensions. At high quantities, no other process matches its cost per part and consistency.

An injection project succeeds or fails on the mould, and HyperFab starts there. First the part design is reviewed for mouldability. Then the mould is designed and built, and sampling and correction continue until the part is approved. Production runs follow, with quality control through to delivery.

HyperFab’s team has taken four polyethylene injection moulds from build supervision to commissioning, and more than 80,000 parts have been produced with them. That experience shows, before the mould is built, where a design is likely to cause trouble in production.

DESIGN FOR MOULDING

Design review before the mould is built

Changing a design on paper costs almost nothing; changing a steel mould is expensive. These points are reviewed before tooling.

Sufficient draft angle on walls, so the part releases without scuffing or sticking.

Uniform wall thickness, so the part does not warp or show sink marks.

Proper ribs and bosses instead of thick, material-heavy walls.

Gate location, parting line and ejector pin positions.

Undercuts and the need for slides or cores in the mould.

Realistic tolerances for each material, allowing for polymer shrinkage.

MATERIALS

Injection moulding materials

The material is recommended based on load, temperature, working environment and part cost.

Polyethylene (PE)

Chemical- and impact-resistant; for caps, containers, fittings and industrial parts.

Polypropylene (PP)

Light and fatigue-resistant; for living hinges, packaging and consumer parts.

ABS

Good-looking surface and solid strength; for device housings and cosmetic parts.

Nylon (PA)

Tough and wear-resistant; for gears, bushings and mechanical parts.

Engineering thermoplastics

Polycarbonate, acetal (POM) and other materials, depending on the part’s use.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Does HyperFab make plastic injection moulds?
Yes. HyperFab designs and builds injection moulds and runs production of plastic parts in a range of materials. The project is with HyperFab from design review to delivery.
How are mould making and injection moulding priced?
There are two main parts: a one-off mould cost and a cost per shot in production. The mould cost depends on part size and complexity, number of cavities and undercuts. The cost per shot depends on part material and weight and cycle time. Both are quoted separately after the design is reviewed.
Which materials do you mould?
Polyethylene, polypropylene, ABS, nylon and other engineering thermoplastics such as polycarbonate and acetal, depending on the part’s use.
At what quantity does injection moulding pay off?
It depends on the mould cost and the price per part, and it usually pays off at high quantities. For smaller runs, 3D printing or CNC machining may be cheaper. HyperFab offers all three and recommends the right process after reviewing the project.
I don’t have a file or drawing. What can I do?
Send a sample part, a photo or your idea. Part design and modelling are available, and the mould is built after you approve the design.
Will I see a sample before production?
Yes. Samples are taken after the mould is built and production starts after your approval. Before tooling, you can also make a prototype with 3D printing.
