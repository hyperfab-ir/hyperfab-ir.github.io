WHAT IT IS

What is the HyperFab price estimator?

The HyperFab price estimator (HyperFab Quote) is the online tool on hyperfab.ir that prices a manufactured part. You upload the part file, choose material, thickness, scale and quantity, and see the production price immediately, with no phone calls, no email back-and-forth and no waiting days for a quote.

It has two calculation engines: an FDM 3D printing engine that analyses STL and 3MF files, and a laser cutting and sheet bending engine that reads DXF files. Both run inside your own browser. To get a price, your file is not sent to any server; it is uploaded only when you finalise the order, and only to make that order.

The estimator’s price is not a rough guess. The printing engine slices the model by height like slicer software and calculates walls, infill and supports separately. The laser engine measures the real cut length, pierce count and bends from the drawing. No rate is hard-coded: every rate, from material price per kilo to the fee per bend, is set from HyperFab’s admin panel.

3D PRINTING ENGINE

How the FDM 3D printing price engine works

A 3D print’s price depends above all on machine time and filament mass. HyperFab’s engine calculates both from your model’s real geometry.

Reads STL and 3MF

The 3D model opens in your browser and its volume, dimensions, surface and cross-sections are extracted. A 3D preview of the part is shown on the same page.

Optimal bed orientation

The engine evaluates different faces of the model and picks the orientation on the build plate that needs less support and prints more reliably.

Support analysis

Overhanging surfaces are detected and the required support volume is estimated; supports cost both material and machine time.

Walls and infill, slice by slice

The model is cut into successive slices. Each slice’s perimeter gives wall volume and its inner area gives infill volume, at the layer height and infill percentage you choose.

Filament mass and print time

Volumes and nozzle speeds give filament mass and real machine time. Top and bottom skins are computed from the change in area between slices, so curved and stepped surfaces are not under-estimated.

Scale, quantity and discount

Changing the scale updates the price instantly without re-uploading. As quantity rises, a stepped volume discount up to a set cap is applied to the unit price.

LASER & BENDING ENGINE

Laser cutting and bending prices from a DXF file

Upload a DXF at 1:1 scale in millimetres and choose sheet material and thickness. Each part of the price comes from a direct measurement of the drawing.

Price componentHow it is measured from the drawing
CuttingTotal cut length × sheet thickness, at a rate specific to each material, thickness and assist gas (oxygen or nitrogen)
PiercingNumber of points where the laser must pierce the sheet to start each contour
BendingNumber of bends and the length of each bend line, plus press-brake setup
EngravingLength of engraving paths on the part
MaterialSheet weight from part area, thickness and material density, times price per kilo
SetupA one-off fee per order, spread across the quantity rather than charged in full on every part
QuantityStepped discounts by quantity
Note: because setup is spread across the quantity, the unit price genuinely drops at 20 or 40 parts; it does not depend on the discount alone.
DFM CHECKS

Automatic manufacturability checks before you order

The laser estimator does more than price. It reviews the drawing like an experienced operator and marks every problem on the drawing itself.

Duplicate overlapping lines: the same path would be cut twice and ruin the edge. Duplicates are excluded from the price.

Open contour: an outline that does not close, so the part will not drop out of the sheet.

Self-intersecting paths and no closed contour in the drawing.

Hole smaller than sheet thickness: will not cut cleanly with a laser.

Web thinner than the thickness: a narrow strip that warps or burns during cutting.

Part larger than a standard sheet and machine capacity.

Text, hatching and dimension lines left in the cutting drawing by mistake.

Hole too close to a bend line: it will distort during bending.

Short flange: an edge too short to sit on the bending tool.

Sheet too thick to bend and bend longer than the press brake.

Missing bending drawing when the part has bends.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How does HyperFab calculate a 3D printing price?
HyperFab’s estimator analyses your STL or 3MF file in your browser. It places the model in the best orientation, estimates the supports needed, calculates wall and infill volume slice by slice, and derives filament mass and print time from these. The price comes from these two values and current rates, with a stepped discount for larger quantities.
How do I get an online laser cutting quote?
Upload the part’s DXF file at 1:1 scale in millimetres on hyperfab.ir and choose sheet material and thickness. The estimator measures cut length, pierce count, bends, engraving and sheet weight from the drawing and shows the price immediately.
Is my file sent to a server to calculate the price?
No. Analysis and pricing run entirely in your own browser. The file is uploaded only when you finalise the order, and used only to make that order.
Is the estimator’s price final?
The estimator’s price comes from your file’s real geometry and current rates, and it is the basis of the order. If the technical review before production finds an issue, HyperFab contacts you before production starts.
Does it give instant prices for resin printing and CNC machining?
No. Resin printing and CNC machining depend on factors that need an engineer’s review, such as resin print orientation or the tooling and number of CNC operations. You upload the file and a price and lead time are quoted after review.
Does the price estimator cost anything?
No. Getting a price is free and creates no obligation. You sign in to a HyperFab account only to place the final order.
Why does print time determine a 3D printing price?
In 3D printing, the main cost is the time the machine spends building the part, not the weight of the plastic. Two parts of the same weight but different geometry can have very different print times and therefore very different prices. That is why HyperFab’s estimator calculates print time from the model’s real geometry.
Is there a minimum order?
There is no minimum quantity; one part is accepted. A minimum order value may apply, and the estimator shows it clearly as a separate line.
